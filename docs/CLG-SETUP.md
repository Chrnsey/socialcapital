# Setting up the CLG

Working notes, not legal advice. The point of this document is to do the
thinking *before* paying a solicitor, so their time is spent checking rather
than interviewing you.

---

## Why a CLG and not something else

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **CLG** | ✅ What charities in Ireland almost always are. No shares, no profit distribution, limited liability for directors, credible to funders and schools. |
| Unincorporated association | ❌ No limited liability — you'd be personally liable. Unthinkable for an organisation working near children. |
| Private LTD | ❌ Has shares and shareholders. Wrong signal to schools, and charitable status doesn't fit. |
| Charitable trust | ❌ Unusual for a service-delivery organisation. |

Observed on the Charities Register: **Junior Achievement / Young Enterprise
Ireland (RCN 20035859)** — the closest Irish comparator — is a CLG.

---

## Order of operations

**1. Find your second director.** Nothing else can start. A CLG needs two
minimum. Boardmatch Ireland exists for this.

**2. Decide the charitable objects.** The substantive work, and only you can
do it. Draft below.

**3. Check the name is available** on the CRO register, and on the Charities
Register.

**4. Start from the Charities Regulator's model constitution.** Do not draft
from scratch. Their model wording is what they expect to see, and deviating
from it invites questions. Fill in the objects and the discretionary parts.

**5. Then the solicitor.** Reviewing your draft costs a fraction of drafting
from nothing, and you'll have already made the decisions they'd otherwise
charge you to extract.

**6. File Form A1 + constitution** with the CRO, online via CORE.

**7. Apply to the Charities Regulator.** Months, so start as soon as the
company exists.

**8. Revenue CHY** tax exemption, after charitable status.

**9. Open a bank account.** Start early — charity accounts in Ireland are
notoriously slow.

## Draft objects clause — for the solicitor to check

Under the Charities Act 2009, the relevant charitable purposes are **the
advancement of education** and **the integration of those who are
disadvantaged and the promotion of their full participation in society**.
Junior Achievement registered under the latter, among others.

> The main object for which the Company is established is to advance the
> education of students attending second-level schools in the State, and in
> particular students attending schools participating in the DEIS programme
> or otherwise experiencing educational disadvantage, by:
>
> (a) providing information about careers, and about the education, training
> and apprenticeship pathways leading to them;
>
> (b) facilitating access to volunteer mentors drawn from a broad range of
> occupations, sectors and entry routes;
>
> (c) arranging talks, visits and information sessions in schools;
>
> (d) undertaking and publishing research on educational and occupational
> opportunity.

The model constitution supplies the rest — the income and property clause,
the winding-up clause, and the restriction on distributions.

## Decisions to make before you sit down with anyone

**Name.** "Social Capital Ireland CLG"? Check availability. A charity may
apply to omit "Company Limited by Guarantee" from its name.

**Registered office.** A physical Irish address. Your home is permitted.

**Directors.** Minimum two. At least one EEA-resident, or a Section 137 bond.
Pick people who will read the papers and disagree with you — a board of
friends who defer is weaker than it looks, to funders and to the Regulator.

**Company secretary.** Can be one of the directors where there are two or more.

**Members / guarantors.** At least one, guaranteeing a nominal amount,
typically €1.

**Financial year end.** Pick one that doesn't collide with the school year.

## The four questions to actually ask the solicitor

1. **"Can the constitution allow for a director to be employed by the charity
   in future, with proper governance?"** Some model wording bars paying any
   trustee permanently. If you may ever want this to be your paid job, raise
   it *before* filing — amending afterwards means going back to the Regulator.

2. **"Do these objects support charging schools a fee later?"** Free for DEIS
   schools is the current plan, but don't have it drafted so that any future
   earned income sits outside the objects.

3. **"What do we need in place given we work with children?"** Child
   Safeguarding Statement under the Children First Act 2015, and Garda
   vetting via a registered relevant organisation.

4. **"What are our filing obligations and when?"** CRO annual return, Charities
   Regulator annual report, and whether an audit or independent examination
   applies at our income level.
