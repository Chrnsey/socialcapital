About

Ireland · started 2026

Social Capital is a directory of working people who have agreed to answer questions from students and recent school leavers about their job and how they got there.

Why it exists

Some of the people I met in my first job already had everything planned out. They had laid out the groundwork, with law firm trainee contracts waiting for them; these sons and daughters of solicitors knew exactly what to do to get a traineeship at a big firm (and it probably helped that they knew a few people inside those firms too). I had supportive parents, but in this job market it isn't only about wanting it. It helps to know someone who'll give you that first chance. Social Capital is for people who don't have the same phonebook.

— Jack, founder, Social Capital

Careers advice isn't really what's missing. What's missing is knowing someone. Which exams actually matter? Is a course worth the points? Who will read your CV? Does an apprenticeship pay? That kind of information gets passed on through families and contacts, so some students get it and others don't, and that has nothing to do with ability.

What we do

We focus on three things.

  • We explain the routes. Apprenticeships, PLCs, the CAO, studying in Europe, and the grants that often get missed. The guides are public, so anyone can use them without logging in.
  • We give schools a directory. Registered schools can browse profiles of professionals who have volunteered, and we arrange talks and introductions.
  • We stay useful after school. Once someone is over 18, whether they're still in sixth year or left years ago, they can get their own account and contact mentors directly. That's usually when an internship or a first job is on the line.

What we don't do

  • We don't let cost keep a school out. The first five schools to register are founding schools and stay free permanently. After that there's an annual fee of €200, but it stays free for any school where the money is genuinely the obstacle. Just let us know.
  • We don't hold data about children. Students browse through their school, and nobody under 18 has an account here.
  • We don't give mentors' personal details to students. Mentors' private contact details can't be read by this website at all, and adults over 18 only see what a mentor has chosen to share.
  • We don't invent people. Every profile is a real person who said yes. If the directory looks short, that's because it is.

Where we are

We're early. The guides are written and open to everyone. The mentor directory is being built by asking people one at a time, so it will look thin for a while before it looks useful. We'd rather be upfront about that than pad it out.

We are in the process of incorporating as a Company Limited by Guarantee and applying for charitable status. Registered name, address and company number will appear here once that is complete, along with our directors and our child safeguarding statement.

Who is behind it

Founded by Jack, working in Dublin. Directors and advisers will be listed here as the company is formed.

If you have experience in governance, education, youth work or child protection and you'd like to get involved, the board is being formed now, so get in touch.

Getting involved

  • Working? Put your name forward as a mentor. An hour a term is enough.
  • Teaching? Register your school.
  • Running a company? List staff or fund a term of talks.
  • Left school and over 18? Get an account.
