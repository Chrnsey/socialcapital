Electrician

Get paid from your first year with no fees, and be fully qualified in about four. After that you can work for a company or for yourself.

Electricians install and repair electrical systems in houses, building sites and factories. There is also a growing requirement for work on solar panels, heat pumps and car chargers. It's a skilled trade with a formal qualification at the end, and people do fail the exams.

Once qualified you can work for a company, set up your own, or move up to running sites.

Ways in

There is more than one. None of these is the "proper" one.

Apprenticeship

The main route

Find an employer who'll register you, then you alternate between working for them and blocks of training. You're paid the whole way through.

How long
About four years
What it costs
No fees to speak of

PLC first, then apprenticeship

If you can't find an employer yet

A year of electrical training makes employers far more likely to take you on. It's a common route if you finish sixth year without anything lined up.

How long
A year, usually
What it costs
Low or nothing

Later on

No age limit

Some people start at twenty-eight after five years in a different job.

How long
Same
What it costs

What you need

A Leaving Cert at a basic grade, or an approved equivalent, or an employer who wants to take you on anyway. Maths and a practical subject help, but high points aren't what matters here. Entry requirements change, so check the current ones before you rely on this.

What you earn on the way

You're paid a percentage of the qualified rate from year one, and it goes up each year. By the end you've been paid for four years and you owe nothing in fees.

What people get wrong

“It's what you do if you're not academic.”

People fail the exams every year, so it isn't an easy option.

“You need to know someone.”

It helps, which is why this site exists. Ringing local firms yourself also works, and very few people do it.

“There's no progression.”

There's plenty. Electricians move into supervision, contracting, inspection and teaching, or run their own company, and some earn more than people with degrees.
