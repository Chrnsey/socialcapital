What's involved

Probably less than you think

There's no script and no quota. Most mentors do a lot less than they expected.

You decide who can reach you

Your profile goes up under your industry, but the email and phone number you give us stay private. Anyone under 18 can only reach you through their school. You can also choose to let over-18s contact you directly (sixth years who have turned 18, and past pupils) by sharing a LinkedIn profile, an email address, or both.

Talks in schools

Some mentors give a short talk once a year. It's entirely optional, and if you say yes we'll handle the arranging.

Junior staff are just as valuable

If you're only a few years in, students will relate to you in a way they might not with a managing director. If you're fifteen years in, you can give them the bigger picture. We need both, and junior mentors are harder to find.

Step back whenever you like

Answer a couple of questions a term, or do more later if you want to. You can step back at any time.

Why this exists

Some of the people I met in my first job already had everything planned out. They had laid out the groundwork, with law firm trainee contracts waiting for them; these sons and daughters of solicitors knew exactly what to do to get a traineeship at a big firm (and it probably helped that they knew a few people inside those firms too). I had supportive parents, but in this job market it isn't only about wanting it. It helps to know someone who'll give you that first chance. Social Capital is for people who don't have the same phonebook.
— Jack, founder, Social Capital
