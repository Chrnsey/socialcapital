What it actually involves

Less than you are imagining

No script, no quota, nobody counting. Most mentors do considerably less than they expected, and it still works.

You decide who can reach you

Your profile goes up under your industry. Your personal email and phone number never do. School students can only reach you through us. Separately you can choose to be contactable by former students over 18 — most people share a LinkedIn profile for that.

Twenty minutes in a classroom

Some mentors do this once a year. Entirely optional, and if you say yes we deal with the arranging.

Three years in is not too soon

If you are early on, students believe you in a way they do not quite believe the managing director. If you are fifteen years in, you have the wider view. Both are wanted and the first is harder to come by.

You can stop

Answer a couple of questions a term, or do more later if you feel like it. You can step back whenever and nobody will ring you about it.

Why this exists

Some of the people I met in my first job already had everything planned out. They had laid out the groundwork, with law firm trainee contracts waiting for them; these sons and daughters of solicitors knew exactly what to do to get a traineeship at a big firm (and it probably helped that they knew a few people inside those firms too). I had supportive parents, but in this job market it isn't only about wanting it. It helps to know someone who'll give you that first chance. Social Capital is for people who don't have the same phonebook.
— Jack, founder, Social Capital
