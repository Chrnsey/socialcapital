Paying for it · Higher Education Access Route

HEAR

A way into college on reduced points if your circumstances qualify. A lot of the students it was set up for never apply.

HEAR offers college places on reduced points, plus extra support once you're in, to school leavers from socio-economically disadvantaged backgrounds. The universities run it through the CAO.

There's a real chance you qualify. It's assessed on your household, so you need to apply and show your circumstances rather than hoping someone notices them.

This might be the most useful thing on this website. Every year people who would have qualified miss out. It's nearly always for one of three reasons: nobody mentioned it, the paperwork looked like too much, or they assumed it was meant for someone worse off.

What you actually get

A place on lower points than the published requirement, and support once you're there, such as orientation, study help and in some cases money. It's not a lesser degree. It's the same course, the same exams and the same degree, and nobody can tell afterwards.

The deadline is earlier than you think

HEAR goes in with your CAO application, but its closing date is well before the main CAO one. Supporting documents are due even earlier. Miss it and you'll have to wait a year. Look up this year's dates in the autumn of sixth year, not in the spring.

The paperwork is the real hurdle

You'll need proof of income and evidence of your circumstances. None of it is hard, it's just tedious, and that's what puts people off. Start early and ask your guidance counsellor to help. It's exactly what they're there for, and most will be glad you asked.

Applying costs you nothing

If it turns out you're not eligible, your normal CAO application carries on as usual. Applying can't leave you worse off.

What people get wrong

“It's for people in real hardship, not me.”

It looks at several things, not just income, and plenty of ordinary working households qualify. Let them decide if you're eligible. Don't rule yourself out.

“Everyone will know I got in on reduced points.”

They won't. It isn't on your degree and nobody in your lectures is told.

“The school handles it.”

You handle it. The school can help with documents, but nobody will do it for you or chase you to get it done.
