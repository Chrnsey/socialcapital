# Child Safeguarding Statement

*Draft for review. Not yet adopted and not yet published on the site.*

> **Before this goes live, three things must be filled in:** the registered
> name and address of the company, the name of the Relevant Person, and the
> date the directors adopt it. They are marked below.

Social Capital Ireland is required to have this statement under **section 11
of the Children First Act 2015**. It sets out what we do, where a child could
come to harm through what we do, and what we have put in place to prevent it.

We want this statement to be used, not just filed away. If anything in it is
wrong or no longer describes what actually happens, please tell us so we can
correct it.

## 1. What the service is

Social Capital Ireland connects students in DEIS schools with working
professionals who have volunteered to answer questions about their job and
how they got it.

**Who holds an account, and who doesn't.** This is the most important part of
how the service is set up.

- **Nobody under 18 has an account.** A student or past pupil aged 18 or over
  registers themselves using an invitation from their school. The school
  confirms the person is 18 or over before sending it, the invitation only
  works for one email address, and registration is refused outright if the
  date of birth given makes them under 18. That check is in the database, not
  in the website, so it can't be skipped.
- **Schools hold a staff account.** A member of staff signs in and sees the
  directory.
- **Students under 18 have no account, and we hold no data about them.** They
  come across the service through their school: a member of staff shows them
  the directory, prints a profile, or arranges an introduction.

So the people we hold records about are adults. But the people the service is
*for* include children (senior cycle students, typically 17 to 19), and the
risk assessment below is written with them in mind, because a child who never
signs in to anything can still be harmed by what we put in front of them.

**The service runs on two levels, and keeping them separate is the main
safeguarding control in this statement.**

### The mentor network: no contact with children

Most of our volunteers never meet or speak to a student.

- Registered school staff sign in and browse a directory of volunteers.
- **Volunteers' personal contact details are not visible to anyone on the
  website**, including signed-in schools. They are held separately and only
  we can read them.
- Where a school wants an introduction, we arrange it with the school, through
  school staff.
- Direct contact between a volunteer and a student or past pupil is **only
  available to people aged 18 or over** who have registered using an
  invitation from their school.

### School talks: a small, named group

A separate and deliberately small group of people visit schools to speak to
students.

- Everyone in this group is **Garda vetted** before their first visit.
- A school visit is arranged with the school, supervised by school staff the
  whole time, and takes place in a normal classroom setting.
- **Nobody in this group is alone with a student at any point.**
- A volunteer in the wider mentor network is not a speaker and does not visit
  schools. Moving from one group to the other requires vetting first.

> As of this draft the speaker group is one person. This is a limitation, not
> how it is meant to work: talks depend on one person's availability, and
> there is nobody to cover an absence.

## 2. Our principles

- A child's welfare comes before this organisation's reputation or
  convenience.
- A concern is reported whether or not we are certain, and whether or not the
  person reporting it is a mandated person.
- Contact between an adult volunteer and a child is supervised by the school,
  or it doesn't happen.
- Nobody is given access to children because they are willing, well known, or
  useful to us.

## 3. Written risk assessment

This is the assessment required by section 11(1)(b). Each risk is a way a
child could be harmed **through our service specifically**, along with what we
do about it.

| Risk of harm | Where it arises | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| A speaker is alone with a student | School visits | Vetting before the first visit; school staff present the whole time; no one-to-one contact; visits in normal classroom settings only |
| A volunteer gets a student's contact details | School visits, or an introduction | Introductions go through school staff. We don't pass student contact details to volunteers, and we don't hold them |
| A student under 18 reaches the direct-contact network | Member registration | Registration needs an invitation from the school, sent to one email address, plus a date of birth that the database checks. The school confirms the person is 18 or over before inviting them, and can remove their access at any time |
| An adult uses the directory to identify and target a student | Signed-in access | Access is restricted to approved schools and their invited members. The directory lists volunteers, not students. No student is listed anywhere on the site |
| Contact moves off our systems, where we can't see it | Any introduction | Volunteers are told in writing that contact with a student goes through the school. A volunteer who approaches a student directly is removed |
| A concern isn't passed on | Any point | A named Relevant Person; reporting to Tusla whether or not the person reporting is a mandated person; reports recorded in writing |
| A volunteer without vetting drifts into a contact role | Growth of the service | Speaker and network roles are kept as separate records. Vetting is required before a first visit, not after |
| Information about a child is handled carelessly | Enquiries from schools | We don't hold student records. Where a school's email mentions a student, it is kept no longer than needed and not shared |
| A volunteer with a history of harming children joins | Recruitment | Vetting for speakers. A reference and a written agreement for all volunteers. No volunteer is published on the strength of an informal recommendation alone |

> **The remaining risk we are most aware of** is the over-18 network. A
> determined seventeen-year-old could be given an invitation by mistake and
> declare a false date of birth. The control sits with the schools who send
> the invitations, and schools are told this.

## 4. Procedures

### Reporting a concern

Anyone with a concern about a child arising from our service reports it to
the Relevant Person named below, who reports it to **Tusla**. If a child is
in immediate danger, **call An Garda Síochána first**, not us.

A concern is reported whether or not the person reporting it is sure, and
whether or not they are a mandated person. We don't investigate, and we don't
decide whether something is serious enough. That is Tusla's job.

> The Tusla Child Protection and Welfare Report form, and the Tusla Portal for
> mandated reports, are the routes we use.

### Mandated persons

Mandated persons are listed in Schedule 2 of the Children First Act 2015.
They are mostly registered professionals, such as teachers, doctors, social
workers, Gardaí and youth workers.

> **We currently have no mandated persons among our volunteers.** Running
> this organisation doesn't make someone a mandated person. The school staff
> we work with generally are, in their own role. None of this reduces our
> obligation to report, and we report as if we were mandated persons.

We keep a list of any mandated persons among our volunteers and review it
along with this statement.

### An allegation against a volunteer

- The person is stood down from any contact role straight away, before
  anything is established either way. This is not a finding against them.
- The report goes to Tusla, and to the Gardaí where a criminal offence may
  have taken place.
- The school is informed.
- We don't carry out our own investigation, and we don't question the child.
- Everything is recorded in writing, with dates.

### Safe recruitment and selection

- **Garda vetting** before a first school visit, for anyone who visits a
  school.
- A reference and a conversation before anyone is published in the
  directory.
- A written agreement making clear that contact with a student goes through
  the school.
- A record of when we met each volunteer and what was agreed.

> We are not yet registered with the National Vetting Bureau. Until we are,
> vetting is arranged through an umbrella body or through the school, and no
> school visit takes place without it.

### Information and training

Every volunteer is given this statement before they are published. Anyone who
visits schools completes Tusla's free **Introduction to Children First**
e-learning module, and we keep a record that they have.

### Records and review

This statement is reviewed **every two years**, or sooner if the service
changes, if an incident happens, or if we start doing something it doesn't
cover. It is displayed publicly on our website and given to any school,
volunteer, parent or student who asks for it.
