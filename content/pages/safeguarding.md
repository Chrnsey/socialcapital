# Child Safeguarding Statement

*Draft for review. Not yet adopted and not yet published on the site.*

> **Before this goes live, three things must be filled in:** the registered
> name and address of the company, the name of the Relevant Person, and the
> date the directors adopt it. They are marked below.

Social Capital Ireland is required to have this statement under **section 11
of the Children First Act 2015**. It sets out what we do, where a child could
come to harm through what we do, and what we have put in place about it.

It is written to be read, not to be filed. If something in it is wrong or no
longer describes what happens, it is worth more to us corrected than tidy.

## 1. What the service is

Social Capital Ireland connects students in DEIS schools with working
professionals who have volunteered to answer questions about their job and
how they got it.

**Who actually uses it.** Senior cycle, and in practice mostly sixth years —
people deciding what to do after school. They are typically 17 or 18,
depending on their birthday and whether they did Transition Year. A
meaningful number of them are therefore children in law, and this statement
is written on that basis. We do not treat the service as an adult one
because most of its users have turned 18.

**The service runs on two levels, and the difference between them is the main
safeguarding control in this statement.**

### The mentor network — no contact with children

Most of our volunteers never meet or speak to a student.

- Registered school staff sign in and browse a directory of volunteers.
- **Volunteers' personal contact details are not visible to anyone on the
  website**, including signed-in schools. They are held separately and only
  we can read them.
- Where a school wants an introduction, it is arranged by us with the school,
  through school staff.
- Direct contact between a volunteer and a former student is available **only
  to people aged 18 or over** who have registered with a code issued to them
  by the school they attended.

### School talks — a small, named group

A separate and deliberately small group of people attend schools to speak to
students.

- Anyone in this group is **Garda vetted** before their first visit.
- A school visit is arranged with the school, supervised by school staff
  throughout, and takes place in a normal classroom setting.
- **Nobody in this group is alone with a student at any point.**
- A volunteer in the wider mentor network is not a speaker and does not
  attend schools. Moving from one to the other requires vetting first.

> As of this draft the speaker group consists of one person. That is a
> limitation, not a design: it means talks are capped by one person's
> availability and there is nobody to cover an absence.

## 2. Our principles

- A child's welfare comes before this organisation's reputation or
  convenience.
- A concern is reported whether or not we are certain, and whether or not the
  person reporting it is a mandated person.
- Contact between an adult volunteer and a child is supervised by the school,
  or it does not happen.
- Nobody is given access to children because they are willing, well known, or
  useful to us.

## 3. Written risk assessment

This is the assessment required by section 11(1)(b). Each risk is a way a
child could be harmed **through our service specifically**, with what we do
about it.

| Risk of harm | Where it arises | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| A speaker is alone with a student | School visits | Vetting before the first visit; school staff present throughout; no one-to-one contact; visits in normal classroom settings only |
| A volunteer obtains a student's contact details | School visits, or an introduction | Introductions go through school staff. We do not pass student contact details to volunteers, and do not hold them |
| A student under 18 reaches the direct-contact network | Former-student registration | Registration needs a code issued by a school to its leavers, plus a declaration of date of birth. Schools control who gets a code |
| An adult uses the directory to identify and target a student | Signed-in access | Access is restricted to approved schools. The directory contains volunteers, not students. No student is listed anywhere on the site |
| Contact moves off our systems, where we cannot see it | Any introduction | Volunteers are told in writing that contact with a student goes through the school. A volunteer who approaches a student directly is removed |
| A concern is not passed on | Any point | A named Relevant Person; reporting to Tusla regardless of whether the reporter is a mandated person; reports recorded in writing |
| An unvetted volunteer drifts into a contact role | Growth of the service | Speaker and network roles are separate records. Vetting is required before a first visit, not after |
| Information about a child is held carelessly | Enquiries from schools | We do not hold student records. Where a school's email mentions a student, it is kept no longer than needed and not shared |
| A volunteer with a history of harming children joins | Recruitment | Vetting for speakers. Reference and a written agreement for all volunteers. No volunteer is published on the basis of an informal recommendation alone |

> **The residual risk we are most conscious of** is the over-18 network. A
> determined seventeen-year-old who obtained a school code could declare a
> false date of birth. The control sits with the schools who issue the codes,
> and schools are told that.

## 4. Procedures

### Reporting a concern

Anyone with a concern about a child arising from our service reports it to
the Relevant Person named below, who reports it to **Tusla**. If a child is
in immediate danger, **An Garda Síochána is called first**, not us.

A concern is reported whether or not the person reporting it is sure, and
whether or not they are a mandated person. We do not investigate. We do not
decide whether something is serious enough. That is Tusla's job.

> Tusla Child Protection and Welfare Report form, and the Tusla Portal for
> mandated reports, are the routes used.

### Mandated persons

Mandated persons are listed in Schedule 2 of the Children First Act 2015 and
are mostly registered professionals — teachers, doctors, social workers,
Gardaí, youth workers.

> **We currently have no mandated persons among our volunteers.** Running
> this organisation does not make someone a mandated person. School staff we
> work with generally are, in their own role. This does not reduce our
> obligation to report, and we report as if we were.

A list of any mandated persons among our volunteers is kept and reviewed with
this statement.

### An allegation against a volunteer

- The person is stood down from any contact role immediately, before anything
  is established either way. This is not a finding against them.
- The report goes to Tusla, and to the Gardaí where a criminal offence may
  have occurred.
- The school is informed.
- We do not carry out our own investigation and we do not question the child.
- Everything is recorded in writing with dates.

### Safe recruitment and selection

- **Garda vetting** before a first school visit, for anybody who attends a
  school.
- A reference, and a conversation, before anybody is published in the
  directory.
- A written agreement setting out that contact with a student goes through
  the school.
- A record of when we met each volunteer and what was agreed.

> We are not yet registered with the National Vetting Bureau. Until we are,
> vetting is arranged through an umbrella body or through the school, and no
> school visit takes place without it.

### Information and training

Every volunteer is given this statement before they are published. Anyone who
attends schools completes Tusla's free **Introduction to Children First**
e-learning module, and we keep a record that they have.

### Records and review

This statement is reviewed **every two years**, or sooner if the service
changes, if an incident occurs, or if we start doing something it does not
describe. It is displayed publicly on our website and given to any school,
volunteer, parent or student who asks for it.
